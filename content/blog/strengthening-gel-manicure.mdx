"Strengthening manicure" gets searched a lot, so here's a direct answer: our regular [Russian Gel-Overlay Manicure](/blog/russian-manicure-explained) is the strengthening option — it's not a separate treatment, it's what a gel overlay does by design.

## What actually happens structurally

A gel overlay is a layer of hard gel bonded directly to your natural nail plate, cured under UV/LED light. While it's on, that layer does two real things for the nail underneath:

- **It acts as a rigid shield against everyday impact** — the gel absorbs the catch or bend that would otherwise stress the nail plate directly, which is the main reason nails under a well-applied overlay chip and split less than bare nails doing the same daily tasks.
- **It stops you from picking at or over-filing your own nail**, since there's a gel layer between your daily habits and the nail plate itself.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't chemically change or "feed" the nail underneath — any strengthening effect is protective, not nutritional. If your nails are thin or peeling from a health or nutrition issue rather than mechanical damage, a topical treatment (gel included) only helps the outermost layer; the actual fix is elsewhere. What we can promise is that our process doesn't make it worse: the [dry-cuticle technique](/blog/russian-manicure-explained) and always [soaking off rather than prying](/blog/safe-gel-removal-explained) mean the nail plate stays in the same condition appointment after appointment.

## If your nails are already damaged or thin

Start with a [proper removal](/blog/safe-gel-removal-explained) and a short bare recovery window if you're coming off a previous set, then a Russian Gel-Overlay once your nail bed feels solid again — the protective effect works best applied to a nail that's already stable, not as a first fix for one that isn't.
