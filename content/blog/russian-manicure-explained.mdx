If you've searched "Russian manicure" and come away more confused than before, you're not alone — the name describes a *technique*, not a specific polish or shape, and it gets used loosely online. Here's what it actually means, why it holds up longer than a standard manicure, and what to expect if you book one.

## The technique, specifically

A standard manicure usually removes cuticle tissue with a wet cut — soaking the nail, then trimming loose skin with a cuticle nipper. A Russian manicure does the opposite: the entire cuticle and sidewall area is cleaned dry, using an e-file (electric nail drill) with fine bits, before any polish or gel goes on.

That difference sounds small, but it changes the result in two concrete ways:

- **The cuticle line is cleaner.** Dry work lets a trained technician see exactly how much dead skin to remove without over-softening it first, which is what usually causes an uneven or "hacked" cuticle edge.
- **The gel bonds to the nail plate itself, not to leftover cuticle.** Any cuticle tissue left on the nail plate is where gel polish typically lifts first. Removing it fully — not just trimming the visible edge — is the main reason a Russian manicure's gel tends to outlast a standard one.

## Is it safe? Does an e-file damage your nails?

This is the honest concern behind most of the "is Russian manicure bad for you" searches, and it's a fair one — an e-file in inexperienced hands absolutely can thin the nail plate or irritate the skin. The technique itself isn't the risk; the skill and care behind it is.

## How it compares to a standard gel manicure

| | Standard gel manicure | Russian manicure |
|---|---|---|
| Cuticle prep | Wet cut, cuticle nipper | Dry work with an e-file |
| Typical wear | 2–3 weeks before lifting starts | Often close to a full month with proper aftercare |
| Best for | Quick upkeep, sensitive cuticles | Anyone who wants the cleanest possible finish and the longest wear |

Neither is "better" in the abstract — a Russian manicure is a specific tool for a specific outcome: a longer-wearing, more precise result, at the cost of a slightly longer, more meticulous appointment.
