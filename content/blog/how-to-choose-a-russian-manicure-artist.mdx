If you are new to the technique, start with [what a Russian manicure is and how it works](/blog/russian-manicure-explained).

## 1. Is the cuticle work really dry?

A Russian manicure cleans the cuticle and sidewalls dry, with an e-file, instead of soaking the hand and cutting with nippers. If the first step at a salon is a water soak, it is a different service with the same name on the menu.

**Our answer:** yes, dry cuticle work on every Russian manicure. Our pedicure is dry too, with no foot bath.

## 2. Is the artist trained in this specific technique?

An e-file in untrained hands can thin the nail plate or irritate the skin. Ask whether the artist studied the Russian method, not just "nails" in general, and how long the appointment takes. Done properly, the work cannot be rushed.

**Our answer:** our artists train specifically in Russian manicure and structured gel technique. The Russian Gel-Overlay Manicure takes about 2 to 2.5 hours.

## 3. Are the artist and the salon licensed?

Nail technicians in California need a license from the state Board of Barbering and Cosmetology, and the license should be displayed. You can also look a license up on the board's website. It is a thirty-second check and it matters more than any photo on Instagram.

## 4. What happens to the tools between clients?

Metal tools should be cleaned, disinfected and sterilized, then stored sealed until the next client. Files and buffers should be single-use. Ask to see it. A good artist is glad to show you.

**Our answer:** metal tools are sterilized and stored in sealed pouches, and files and buffers are single-use, every visit.

## 5. What is on your nails, and what is not?

Ask what the service is actually made of. Some "Russian manicures" are finished with acrylic, which changes how the nail wears and how it comes off.

**Our answer:** gel only, on every service. We do not offer acrylic at all.

## 6. How long should it last?

A clean cuticle line matters because gel lifts first where it sits on leftover cuticle. A well-done set should grow out, not chip off. Be careful with salons that promise a specific number of weeks without seeing your nails.

**Our answer:** we ask clients to rebook no later than 4 weeks after the last visit so the set always looks neat. If something is not right, tell us within 48 hours and we fix it free within 14 days. See [how the 2-week guarantee works](/blog/2-week-guarantee-explained).

## 7. What does it cost, and why?

Prices vary a lot, and a very low price for a 2-hour dry manicure usually means the time was cut somewhere. Ask what is included and whether the price depends on the artist.

**Our answer:** the Russian Gel-Overlay Manicure is $99 to $109 depending on the artist's level, and the Gel Nail Extension is $150 (about 3 hours). Current prices are always on our [prices page](/prices).
