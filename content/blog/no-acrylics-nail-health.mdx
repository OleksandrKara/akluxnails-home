Acrylic is still the most common nail-extension material in the US, so it's a fair question when a salon simply doesn't offer it. Here's the actual reasoning, not just a marketing line.

## What acrylic does to a natural nail

Acrylic is a liquid-and-powder polymer applied over (or to extend) the nail, then shaped and buffed hard once it sets. Two things make it riskier for long-term nail health specifically:

- **It bonds very aggressively.** Removing it properly requires soaking in acetone or filing it off — either takes real time and skill; rushed removal (common at high-volume salons) peels off layers of the natural nail plate along with the product.
- **It's rigid.** A natural nail flexes slightly with daily use. A hard acrylic overlay doesn't flex with it, so any impact transfers stress straight to the nail bed instead of absorbing it.

Neither of these makes acrylic inherently "bad" — done and removed correctly, it's a normal, common choice. It's a maintenance-and-removal risk, not a chemical one.

## What we use instead: hard gel and gel polish, always

At AK.LUX.NAILS every manicure — [Russian Gel-Overlay Manicure](/blog/russian-manicure-explained), Gel Nail Extension, or a simple gel polish with no extension — uses hard gel and gel polish only. The practical differences:

| | Acrylic | Hard gel / gel polish |
|---|---|---|
| Flexibility | Rigid | Flexes closer to a natural nail |
| Removal | Soak-off or heavy filing | Soaks off cleanly with acetone |
| Odor during application | Strong (liquid monomer) | Minimal |
| Extension strength | Very strong, best for max length | Strong, slightly more natural feel |

If you specifically want maximum length or the most rigid, damage-resistant tip, acrylic genuinely does that better — we just don't think that trade-off belongs in a nail-health-first studio, so we've built the whole menu around gel instead. ## Does gel actually protect your nails better?

Only if it's removed properly, which is true of both materials — the technique matters more than the product. What we control on our end: every gel service is soaked off, never peeled or pried, and paired with the same dry-cuticle technique described in our [Russian manicure guide](/blog/russian-manicure-explained), so the nail plate underneath stays in the same condition appointment after appointment rather than getting thinner over time.
