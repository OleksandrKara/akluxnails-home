If your natural nail is cracked, split, or thinned out and you still want length, the extension itself isn't the first step — the prep work is. Here's what that actually involves as part of a normal [Gel Nail Extension](/blog/gel-nail-extensions-explained) appointment.

## Why damaged nails need prep first

Building a gel extension straight over a crack or a thin, flexible section just transfers stress to the weakest point of the natural nail — the extension can look fine while the damage underneath gets worse. The order matters: stabilize the natural nail first, then extend.

## What that prep actually looks like

- **Assess before shaping.** A crack near the free edge is handled differently than one closer to the cuticle, and a thin nail plate from a previous acrylic or gel removal (see [what removal does short-term](/blog/safe-gel-removal-explained)) sometimes just needs a short recovery window before any product goes on at all.
- **Reinforce the weak point.** Depending on where the damage is, that means gently buffing and sealing a split before it runs further, or building the gel base slightly thicker over a thin section rather than applying uniform pressure across a nail that isn't uniformly strong.
- **Then extend as normal**, using the same gel tip or sculpted form and gel-polish finish as any other extension.

## This isn't a separate booking

It's judgment applied during a regular extension appointment, not an add-on with its own price — tell us at booking that you're dealing with a damaged or thin nail so we can plan the right amount of time, since repair work like this takes longer than shaping a healthy nail.

If the damage is more extensive — several nails, or a nail that's clearly still too thin to build on — we'll tell you honestly if a bare recovery period first is the better call, rather than extending over something that isn't ready.
