If you've worn acrylic for a while and you're considering a switch, the process itself matters more than the decision — done right, it's straightforward; rushed, it's the main way people damage their natural nail plate.

## Start with a proper removal, not a fresh set over old product

We don't offer acrylic, so switching starts with taking your existing set off completely and correctly — soaked off with acetone and gently worked free, never filed down aggressively or pried off. See our [no-acrylic explainer](/blog/no-acrylics-nail-health) for why removal technique, not the material itself, is where most of the real damage risk actually lives.

## Give your natural nail a short recovery window

Your nail underneath has been covered for a while, so it's normal for it to look thinner or feel more flexible right after removal — that's the natural nail adjusting, not new damage. A week or two of bare recovery (cuticle oil daily, no immediate reapplication) gives it a chance to firm back up before your first gel service.

## What gel feels like by comparison

Gel cures under UV/LED light into a noticeably more flexible layer than acrylic — it moves with your nail instead of resisting it, which is the main thing returning acrylic clients comment on. You have the same shape/length choices as before: a [Gel Nail Extension](/blog/gel-nail-extensions-explained) for added length, or a [Russian Gel-Overlay](/blog/russian-manicure-explained) if your natural nail is a length you're happy with already.

## What we'd tell you at your first visit

Bring your current set in as-is — don't try to remove it yourself first. A professional soak-off is faster, gentler on the nail plate, and lets us actually see the condition of your natural nail before recommending what to book next.
