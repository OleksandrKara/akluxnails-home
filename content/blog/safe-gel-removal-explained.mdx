Gel polish and gel overlays are popular because they hold up for weeks without chipping — but that same durability means removal technique matters more than almost anything else in the whole service.

## Why removal is where the real risk is

Peeling or picking off gel takes the top layers of your natural nail plate with it, since gel bonds directly to the nail surface. That's true whether it was applied at our studio or anywhere else — the material isn't the risk, impatience with the removal is.

## What proper removal actually looks like

A real soak-off, done right:

- Light buffing of the top coat first, never aggressive filing straight down to the nail
- Acetone soak, wrapped or in a bowl, for as long as the product actually needs to release
- Gentle lifting once it's ready — if it resists, it soaks longer, it doesn't get pried
- A finishing buff and cuticle oil once your natural nail is bare again

## What your nail looks like right after

Expect some visible thinness or extra flexibility immediately after a removal — that's the top layer of the nail plate that's naturally a little worn from weeks of wear, not damage from the removal itself, assuming it was soaked off properly. It firms back up within a couple of weeks with normal cuticle care.
